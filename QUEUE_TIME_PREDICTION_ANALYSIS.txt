1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY: WHAT YOUR TECHNICAL ADVISER MEANS
================================================================================
Your technical adviser suggested that once an order is verified at the counter,
a timer should start counting upwards, and that this recorded elapsed time should
be used as the dataset for predictive analytics (Estimated Waiting Time / EWT).

The underlying logic of your adviser is CORRECT in queuing theory:
  * You CANNOT start measuring the shop queue from the customer's online booking 
    time. A customer might book 2 days in advance or get stuck in traffic. 
  * The actual physical queue at the laundromat only begins the moment the customer 
    presents their clothes at the counter and staff confirms the order (verified_at).
  * Therefore, verified_at is the true baseline timestamp of entering the queue.

HOWEVER, where the confusion and danger lies is in how the END of that timer is 
measured. If you rely on staff to manually click "Finished" or "Complete Cycle" 
in the POS whenever they notice it:
  * That is NOT a professional engineering move.
  * It introduces catastrophic human latency into your dataset, which destroys 
    the statistical accuracy of your predictive analytics.

2. THE FLAW WITH PURELY MANUAL "POS FINISH" TAGGING
================================================================================
If the system simply counts upwards from verification until staff clicks a button:

A. HUMAN LATENCY & FORGETFULNESS:
   - Example: A 35-minute wash cycle finishes at 2:35 PM.
   - The staff is busy assisting 3 walk-in customers or taking a meal break.
   - At 4:15 PM, the staff finally remembers to tap "Finished" on the POS screen.
   - The recorded cycle time in the database is now 1 hour and 40 minutes (100 mins) 
     instead of 35 minutes!

B. DATA POISONING ("GARBAGE IN, GARBAGE OUT" IN MACHINE LEARNING):
   - Machine Learning models (like Linear Regression, Random Forest, or Moving Average) 
     learn patterns from past historical records.
   - If your training dataset contains wildly distorted durations (some 35 mins, 
     some 90 mins, some 150 mins) simply due to staff delays in clicking a screen, 
     your model's error metrics (MAPE - Mean Absolute Percentage Error) will blow up.
   - During your thesis/capstone defense, the panel can easily invalidate your AI by 
     asking:
     "What if the staff forgot to tap the button for 2 hours? Does that mean your 
      predictive model assumes washing took 2 hours?"

3. THE PROFESSIONAL & PRACTICAL SOLUTION: TWO-STAGE METRIC ARCHITECTURE
================================================================================
In commercial laundromats and enterprise ERPs, operations are broken down into 
distinct, measurable stages:

   [Customer Verified at Counter]  <-- verified_at (Queue Clock Starts)
              |
              |  STAGE 1: QUEUE WAITING TIME (Variable / The true target of AI)
              v  Actual Wait Time = (started_at - verified_at)
   [Loaded into Drum & Cycle Started] <-- started_at (Machine Locks & Runs)
              |
              |  STAGE 2: MACHINE PROCESSING TIME (Deterministic / Hardware Physics)
              v  Physical Cycle Run: Inverter timer (e.g. 35 mins Wash, 40 mins Dry)
   [Cycle Automated End / Drum Unloaded] <-- cycle_ends_at (Auto-Transitions)
              |
              |  STAGE 3: FOLDING & FULFILLMENT (Staff Labor / Optional Service)
              v  Turnaround Time = (ready_for_pickup - cycle_ends_at)
   [Customer Notification / Picked Up]

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 STAGE 1: ACTUAL QUEUE WAITING TIME (The Core Prediction Feature)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- What is variable? The queue line.
- The customer does not ask: "How long does a washing machine spin?" 
  (Everyone knows a machine cycle is ~35-40 minutes).
- The customer asks: "HOW LONG WILL I WAIT BEFORE A MACHINE BECOMES FREE?"
- Formula:
     actual_wait_mins = (started_at - verified_at).total_seconds() / 60.0
- If a machine is immediately free at check-in: actual_wait_mins = 0.
- If all washers are occupied: actual_wait_mins = elapsed time until a drum opened.
- This is the CLEANEST, MOST ACCURATE ground-truth target for your EWT predictor!

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.2 STAGE 2: DETERMINISTIC MACHINE CYCLE COUNTDOWN (Automated, Not Manual)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Commercial washing and drying machines operate on fixed timer boards.
- In Bon Laundry, when staff assigns a machine and clicks "Start Cycle" (or 
  "Start Cycle Immediately" in the verification modal):
  * The machine status is marked 'In Use'.
  * The system computes: cycle_ends_at = cycle_started_at + cycle_duration_mins.
  * An automated countdown runs.
  * When the timer expires, the machine status automatically transitions to 
    'Available' (or 'Pending Unload').
- This completely ELIMINATES human forgetfulness from corrupting the machine time!

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.3 STAGE 3: FULFILLMENT & PICKUP (With Outlier Sanitization)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- For "Wash-Dry-Fold" drop-off orders, staff marks when folding is finished 
  (fulfillment_status = 'Ready for Pickup').
- For predictive models that predict full pickup turnaround:
  * Apply data capping and outlier filtering.
  * Exclude orders left overnight or after operating hours (e.g. > 12 hours) from 
    the active queue ML training dataset so standard operational metrics remain pure.

4. WHY MACHINE LEARNING NEEDS THIS SEPARATION
================================================================================
By separating Waiting Time from Machine Cycle Time, your predictive analytics 
becomes scientifically robust and defensible:

1. Clean Feature Engineering:
   - Features: (Current Active Queue Count, Hour of Day, Day of Week, Service Type, 
     Fleet Utilization Rate).
   - Target Label: actual_wait_mins (Calculated from verified_at to started_at).

2. Mathematical Precision:
   - Total Estimated Time for Customer = Predicted Queue Wait Time + Fixed Machine Duration.
   - Example: 14 mins predicted queue wait + 35 mins wash cycle = 49 mins total EWT.
   - This approach is standard in Operations Research (Erlang C queue models and 
     industrial scheduling algorithms).

5. IMPLEMENTATION IN BON LAUNDRY SYSTEM CODEBASE
================================================================================
The Bon Laundry Station codebase is already structured to support this exact 
clean architecture:

1. Database Schema (`models/schemas.py` - `tbl_queue` & `tbl_appointment`):
   - `verified_at`     : Recorded when staff verifies the order at counter.
   - `started_at`      : Recorded when clothes enter the machine.
   - `actual_wait_mins`: System-calculated exact difference between started_at 
                         and verified_at.
   - `status`          : Transitions cleanly: 'Waiting' -> 'Processing' -> 'Completed'.

2. Real-time Machine Status (`services/machines.py`):
   - `is_machine_available()`
   - `start_machine_cycle()` with deterministic `cycle_ends_at`
   - `refresh_machine_cycles()` automatically checks if cycles ended based on 
     timestamps without waiting for manual human clicks.

3. Counter Verification Modal (`templates/staff/verify_order.html` & `dashboard.html`):
   - Recommends the optimal machine.
   - Has "Start Machine Cycle Immediately" toggle:
     * If laundry is loaded right at check-in: actual_wait_mins = 0, status = 'Processing'.
     * If queued behind other orders: wait timer counts upwards until a drum frees up.

"Sir/Ma'am, inaral po namin nang mabuti yung recommendation niyo tungkol sa 
time tracking mula sa point of verification. Sang-ayon po kami 100% na sa 
`verified_at` (counter check-in) dapat magsimula ang baseline queue time dahil 
doon pa lang pumapasok ang customer sa active physical queue ng shop.

Para naman po maging scientifically accurate ang Predictive Analytics natin at 
hindi maapektuhan ng Human Latency (halimbawa, kapag busy ang staff at late na 
napindot ang 'Finish' sa POS kahit matagal nang tapos ang labada), hinati po 
namin ang time tracking sa dalawang professional layers:

1. Actual Queue Waiting Time (`verified_at` hanggang `started_at`):
   Ito po ang mismong pinapakain natin sa machine learning algorithm para 
   hulaan kung gaano katagal maghihintay sa pila ang susunod na customer bago 
   masalang ang damit sa washer.

2. Machine Processing Time (`started_at` hanggang `cycle_end`):
   Ito po ay naka-tie sa automated countdown ng inverter machine specs 
   (39 mins wash / 40 mins dry). Hindi na ito umaasa sa manual memory ng staff, 
   kaya walang dirty data o human error na sumisira sa predictive model.

Sa ganitong arkitektura po, accurate ang dataset natin, automated ang workflow ng 
staff, at madaling ipagtanggol sa panel dahil sumusunod ito sa standards ng 
Queuing Theory."


FORMAL ENGLISH VERSION (For Presentation Slides & Thesis Manuscript):
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Our system adopts an Event-Driven Multi-Tier Queue Tracking Architecture:

1. Baseline Check-In (`verified_at`):
   Marks the customer's arrival and formal entry into the physical queue.

2. Queue Latency Tracking (`actual_wait_mins`):
   Measures the delta between verification and machine cycle initiation 
   (started_at - verified_at). This serves as the ground-truth regression target 
   for the Estimated Waiting Time (EWT) prediction engine.

3. Deterministic Machine Execution (`cycle_ends_at`):
   Prevents staff human latency from contaminating the machine learning dataset. 
   Machine run durations are governed by automated hardware countdown timers rather 
   than retrospective manual POS inputs.

This separation prevents 'data poisoning,' preserves model integrity (minimizing 
MAPE), and guarantees operational reliability under high-throughput conditions."
