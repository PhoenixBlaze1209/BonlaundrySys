[4] 1. Executive Summary & Architecture Overview
[5] Overview: This functional specification document presents a rigorous technical and operational analysis of the user-facing Customer Account Portal developed for Bon Laundry Station. Operating as a responsive web application tailored for condominium residents at Urban Deca Homes Ortigas and walk-in patrons, the customer portal bridges end-user service demand with on-site commercial operations.
[6] Architectural Purpose: The customer portal is engineered with four primary functional pillars, accessible through both a persistent top navigation bar and a secondary tab strip. These modules comprise: (1) Live Machine Fleet Lobby, (2) Services & Official Rate Card, (3) Step-by-Step Booking Wizard, and (4) My Orders & Live Tracking. Together with real-time telemetry metrics and automated SMS integration, the user interface eliminates common customer friction points such as uncertain wait times, physical counter queues, and fabric misplacement.
[7] 1.1 Global Application Shell & Persistent Navigation Components
[8] Shell Framework: Across all customer portal views, a consistent global application shell provides authenticated identity context, continuous telemetry feedback, and effortless navigation:
[9] Primary Navbar: Top Sticky Navigation Bar: Houses the Bon Laundry brand badge, high-level navigation buttons (My Orders, Book Order, Services & Rates, and Live Fleet Lobby with an active pulsing status dot), the customer profile pill showing Google account verification and email, and the Sign Out button.
[10] Telemetry Hero Banner: Hero Banner with Real-Time Telemetry Strip: A dark navy/teal gradient hero banner that acknowledges the customer's Google-authenticated identity and displays three mission-critical operational KPI metrics: Active Orders (e.g., 1), Ready Washers ratio (e.g., 19/19 machines idle), and Current Wait time (e.g., ~65m Estimated Waiting Time).
[11] Tab Strip: Secondary Tab Navigation: An intuitive secondary tab menu with active blue indicators and numeric badges (e.g., 'My Orders & Live Tracking [1]'), allowing customers to toggle seamlessly between views without triggering disruptive page reloads.
[12] Alert Notifications: System Flash Alerts & Dismissible Toasts: Informative feedback banners displaying sign-in acknowledgment and operational status messages.
[13] Footer: Application Footer: Displays facility copyright, physical branch location (Urban Deca Homes Ortigas Commercial Area), and quick navigational shortcuts to the public landing page and sign-out route.

[18] 2.1 Module Objective & Business Role
[19] Business Purpose: The Live Machine Fleet Lobby provides real-time visibility into the operational state of the physical laundry shop. In traditional laundromats, customers frequently encounter long, unpredictable delays upon arriving on-site with heavy hampers, only to discover that every machine is occupied. The Live Fleet Lobby completely eliminates this asymmetry by streaming the live occupancy, capacity, and cycle state of all 19 commercial laundry machines directly to the customer's device.
[20] Traffic Optimization: By allowing customers to inspect machine availability prior to leaving their residential unit in Urban Deca Homes, the module distributes customer traffic evenly, reduces peak-hour bottlenecking, and encourages self-service adoption.

[24] 2.3 Hardware Fleet Specifications & Telemetry Logic
[25] Fleet Composition: The Bon Laundry Station fleet comprises 19 specialized commercial units structured as follows:
[26] Washer Fleet: 14 Regular Commercial Washers (Machines 1 to 14): Standard 8kg capacity, priced at ₱145 per cycle. Designed for everyday casuals, undergarments, linens, and light garments with an average cycle time of 35-40 minutes.
[27] Titan Dryer Fleet: 5 Titan Heavy-Duty Dryers (Machines 15 to 19): High-capacity 13kg commercial tumbler dryers, priced at ₱240 per cycle. Designed for heavy comforters, bulky towels, bedsheets, and high-volume loads with an average drying duration of 45 minutes.
[28] Cycle Expiry Engine: Automated Status Synchronization: The backend executes `refresh_machine_cycles(db)` whenever the customer portal is accessed. This procedure checks current timestamps against machine cycle end-times, automatically releasing hardware from 'In-Use' to 'Available' once cycles conclude.

[75] 5.3 4-Stage Lifecycle State Machine
[76] State Progression: Every order in the Bon Laundry System transitions through four strictly sequenced operational states:
[77] Stage 1: Stage 1 — Received (Intake & Weighed): Staff inspects garments, confirms package deliverables, records actual weight, and verifies customer contact info at the POS terminal.
[78] Stage 2: Stage 2 — In Queue (Waiting Fleet Slot): The order is issued a sequential queue number (#82) and waits for an available washer matching the load requirements. (Currently depicted as active in Figure 4).
[79] Stage 3: Stage 3 — Processing (Wash & Dry Cycles): The load is placed into an allocated commercial washer or dryer. Cycle countdown timers initiate, and machine state transitions to 'In-Use'.
[80] Stage 4: Stage 4 — Ready (Folded & Packed / Delivered): Garments have concluded high-heat drying, been neatly flat-folded, packed in sealed plastic, and placed in pickup cubbies or dispatched for condo doorstep delivery.
[81] 5.4 Real-Time Synchronization & Cancellation Logic
[82] Polling Engine: The customer portal incorporates an automated polling mechanism targeting `GET /api/customer/my-orders`. Every 10-15 seconds, the background script queries the backend to determine if the order status, queue number, or assigned machine has updated in response to staff floor actions.
[83] Cancellation Safeguards: Order cancellation is constrained by business logic: if an order is in 'Pending' or 'In Queue' status (before washing commences), the customer can click 'Request Cancellation'. This prompts a confirmation modal, invalidates the queue ticket, and restores machine allocation without requiring manager intervention.
